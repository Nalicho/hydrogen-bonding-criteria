Geometric Criteria and Properties of Hydrogen Bonds

Hydrogen bonding is a donor-acceptor interaction involving hydrogen atoms bound to electronegative donors[cite: 4, 5]. Depending on donor-acceptor distances, hydrogen-to-acceptor distances, and interaction angles, hydrogen bonds are categorized into strong, moderate, and weak interactions[cite: 1, 5].

Summary of Geometric Parameters

  • Strong H-bond: Mostly covalent; D···A distance: 220–250 pm ($2.2–2.5\text{ \AA}$); H···A distance: 120–150 pm ($1.2–1.5\text{ \AA}$); Angle: $175^\circ–180^\circ$[cite: 1, 5].
  • Moderate H-bond: Mostly electrostatic; D···A distance: 250–320 pm ($2.5–3.2\text{ \AA}$); H···A distance: 150–220 pm ($1.5–2.2\text{ \AA}$); Angle: $130^\circ–180^\circ$[cite: 1, 5].
  • Weak H-bond: Electrostatic; D···A distance: 320–400 pm ($3.2–4.0\text{ \AA}$); H···A distance: 220–320 pm ($2.2–3.2\text{ \AA}$); Angle: $90^\circ–150^\circ$[cite: 1, 5].
